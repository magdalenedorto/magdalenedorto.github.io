My story, rooted in Mepe

Where curiosity became leadership

Early foundations

When I was a girl in Mepe

My passion for excellence began in Mepe, North Tongu, where I learned that leadership starts with showing up for others.

From Primary 4 through Junior High, I took part in the competitive Fosu Exams and served as Girls Prefect in both primary and JHS. Later, as a YAM Branch Chair, I led self-sponsored and grant-funded community initiatives. I have served as a regional and international youth advocate since 2016.

That early grounding still shapes my interest in international relations and diplomacy: I want to understand how institutions, communities, and young people can work across borders to create fairer opportunities.

My teaching philosophy

Learn to inhabit the language.

Spanish is more than a set of rules. It is belonging, culture, connection, and a new way of thinking.

My teaching philosophy treats Spanish as a way to build cultural connection and belonging. Students do more than memorize rules: they learn to inhabit the language, its culture, and its ways of thinking. Because I understand the doubts that come with starting from scratch, I support each learner as they build confidence, fluency, and independence.

In online lessons, I use technology to bring the Spanish-speaking world closer. The approach combines clear grammar instruction with practical, contextual conversation so learners can use Spanish beyond the classroom.

Every student brings a different experience and pace to learning. I create an inclusive, culturally responsive space where learners feel safe, confidence can grow, and mistakes become part of the learning process.
